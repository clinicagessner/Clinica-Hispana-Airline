# How to Prepare for an Ultrasound: Fasting, Bladder, Clothes

**One patient shows up at our Airline clinic having skipped breakfast. Another has just finished four glasses of water. Both got it right, because their scans call for opposite things.** How you prepare for an ultrasound depends on which part of the body is being looked at, and poor preparation is the most common reason an image comes out less clear than it could have. Here is a scan-by-scan walkthrough, what happens if you arrive unprepared, and a short list of what to put in your bag.

## Start by finding your scan

Clínica Hispana Airline performs four kinds of [ultrasound](/services/ultrasonido). Find yours in the table first:

| Scan | What it looks at | How to arrive |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy (sonogram) | Confirming the pregnancy, the heartbeat, dating the weeks, following development | Full bladder, especially early in pregnancy |
| Pelvic | Uterus and ovaries | Full bladder |
| Abdominal | Liver, gallbladder, kidneys, pancreas and spleen | No food for 6 to 8 hours |
| Thyroid and soft tissue | Nodules or lumps you can feel | No special preparation |

Not sure which one you need? That's fine. We tell you the preparation for your scan when you get here. What really saves you a wasted morning is calling the day before, so you don't eat breakfast when you shouldn't have.

## Abdominal: an empty stomach is everything

Sound waves travel easily through fluid and poorly through air. After a meal your stomach is full and your gut makes gas, and that gas sits in front of the liver, gallbladder or pancreas like a curtain. That's why the recommendation is **no food for 6 to 8 hours** beforehand.

The easiest approach is a morning appointment and a light dinner the night before. A few things that help us get a clean picture:

- **Eat dinner early and keep it low in fat.** A late plate of carnitas or beans with chorizo is still causing trouble at nine the next morning.
- **Skip soda, beer and sparkling water** the evening before. Bubbles are gas.
- **No gum and no cigarettes** while you wait; both make you swallow air.
- **Working nights?** Line the fast up with your sleep and come in right after your shift.

A situation we see often: someone with pain under the right ribs, worried about gallstones, has a coffee and a sweet roll because "that doesn't count." It does. The gallbladder empties when you eat, so it looks smaller and harder to read, and it was the very organ we needed to see.

### Diabetes or daily medications

Fasting doesn't mean dropping your medicines on your own. As a general rule, blood pressure and thyroid pills are taken with a sip of water. Diabetes needs more care: if you use insulin or pills that lower blood sugar, skipping breakfast can push your glucose too low. Call us first and we'll walk you through it, especially if we already follow your [diabetes or other chronic conditions](/services/condiciones-cronicas). Bring a snack to eat as soon as the scan is over.

## Pelvic and early pregnancy: a full bladder

Here it's the reverse. A full bladder pushes the bowel up and out of the way and leaves a pocket of fluid the sound can pass through cleanly to reach the uterus and ovaries. Clinicians call it an acoustic window.

As general guidance, many people are told to drink several glasses of water over the hour before and not to empty their bladder until afterward. The exact amount varies, so ask when you call if you're unsure. Practical tips:

- **Start drinking at home or in the car,** not in the waiting room. A bladder takes a while to fill.
- **Water, not soda or coffee,** which bring on an urgency that's hard to hold.
- **Don't overdo it.** If the pressure turns painful, tell us. Sometimes you can let a little out and carry on.

The pregnancy sonogram works much the same way in the first weeks, while the uterus is still tucked behind the pelvic bone. Later on, once it has grown, a full bladder matters less, and we'll let you know on arrival whether you need one.

If you've only just missed a period and aren't sure you're pregnant, a [pregnancy test](/services/prueba-embarazo) is often the first step, with the ultrasound after.

## Thyroid and lumps: nothing to do

Scans of the thyroid or of a lump under the skin need no fasting and no water. Just keep the area easy to reach. For the neck, leave necklaces, scarves and turtlenecks at home. If the lump is somewhere else, pick clothing that lets you uncover that spot without fully undressing.

## What if I show up unprepared?

It happens every day, and it's fixable:

- **You ate before an abdominal scan.** The clinic's medical team decides whether the image is usable or whether it's better to come back another day fasting. Sometimes enough shows through; sometimes the gas wins, and a properly prepared scan beats a half-readable one.
- **You arrived with an empty bladder for a pelvic scan.** Usually you drink water here and wait until it fills.
- **Nerves sent you to the restroom right before going in.** Same fix: water and a little patience.

Don't assume the visit is wasted. Tell us what you ate or drank and when, and we'll decide together.

## What to wear: two pieces, nothing in the way

Comfortable clothes, ideally **two pieces**: pants or a skirt with a top. That way you only need to lift your shirt or lower your waistband a little instead of changing completely. One-piece dresses and coveralls mean taking almost everything off. Leave jewelry you don't need at home, and if you're coming straight from work in a one-piece uniform, pack a T-shirt.

## What to bring

- **Earlier scans and results.** Past ultrasounds, even from another clinic or from back home, plus recent lab work. Comparing against an old image tells us a lot: whether a cyst has grown, whether a nodule hasn't changed.
- **A list of your medications** with doses, including over-the-counter products and supplements.
- **The first day of your last period.** For pelvic and pregnancy scans we always ask, because it helps make sense of what we see and date the weeks. Save it in your phone before you leave.
- **Any note or order** from whoever recommended the scan, so we know exactly what to look for.
- **Someone to come with you,** if it helps you relax. Plenty of moms bring a partner or their own mom to the pregnancy sonogram.

## What it feels like

You lie on the exam table, gel goes on your skin and the transducer glides over the area. **It does not hurt and causes no cramping.** The gel feels cool at first. With a full bladder, the transducer's pressure on your belly can be uncomfortable for a few minutes, and that's about it. The scan takes ten to twenty minutes depending on what's being checked, and the gel wipes right off. It uses sound waves, not radiation.

While the transducer moves, someone talks you through the images in Spanish or English. If something turns up that calls for another test or a specialist, we tell you and the medical team helps direct the referral.

## When not to wait for an ultrasound

If you have **severe belly pain, a high fever with pain, or vomiting that won't stop**, don't sit in the waiting room fasting: go to the emergency room or call 911. During pregnancy, **heavy bleeding, severe pain or fainting** are emergencies too. And keep in mind that a sonogram doesn't replace prenatal care; it's one piece of it.
