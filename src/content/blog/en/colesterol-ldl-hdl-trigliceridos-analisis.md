# Cholesterol Results: What LDL, HDL and Triglycerides Mean

**The lab sheet comes back with five lines, a few stray letters in the margin (an H here, an L there) and the word "cholesterol" printed three times. Hardly anyone knows where to start, and many people only look at the total.** This article walks through the lipid panel in the order it is usually printed, explains what each figure counts and why two people with the same number can get very different advice.

## First, what a lipid panel is

Cholesterol does not float loose in your blood: it rides inside particles that carry it to and from your tissues. The test measures those particles plus a separate fat that circulates on its own, triglycerides. Results are given in milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL) from an ordinary blood draw from the arm.

At our Airline clinic it is part of our [blood tests](/services/examenes-sangre), and you can ask for it on your own, without an order from another clinic.

## The sheet, line by line

The table sums up the desirable values for most adults age 20 and older according to [MedlinePlus, from the National Institutes of Health](https://medlineplus.gov/cholesterollevelswhatyouneedtoknow.html). These are general references: your personal target may be stricter.

| Line | What it measures | General desirable value |
|---|---|---|
| Total cholesterol | All the cholesterol in circulation added up | Under 200 mg/dL |
| LDL | Cholesterol that sticks to the walls of the arteries | Under 100 mg/dL |
| HDL | Cholesterol picked up from the arteries and carried back to the liver | 60 mg/dL or higher is best; low if under 40 for men or under 50 for women |
| Triglycerides | Fat that comes mostly from food and leftover sugar | Under 150 mg/dL |
| Non-HDL | Total minus HDL: every particle that can clog | Under 130 mg/dL |

### LDL: the one that builds plaque

This is the line that weighs most in decisions. When there is too much of it, LDL particles work their way into the artery wall and, over the years, form plaque that narrows the path for blood. It does not hurt, you cannot feel it and it gives no warning while it grows.

One detail that trips people up: many labs do not measure LDL directly but calculate it from the other three values. So when triglycerides come back very high, that calculation becomes less reliable, and the test may need to be repeated or measured another way.

### HDL: the only line where L is the bad news

On almost every line of a lab report, the letter H (high) is the one to worry about. HDL works the other way around: an L next to it means there is too little of the "cleanup" cholesterol, and that adds risk. A high HDL is a good sign, but on its own it does not cancel out a very high LDL; the two are read together.

### Triglycerides: the line that reflects last night's dinner

Triglycerides rise and fall much faster than the rest. A heavy meal the night before, a few beers over the weekend or several sodas a day show up in this number. That is why you are asked to come fasting, 8 to 12 hours without food, though water is fine.

When high triglycerides come with a wide waistline, raised glucose or high blood pressure, the pattern often points to insulin resistance, and it makes sense to check blood sugar too.

### Non-HDL: the line almost nobody reads

Some labs already print it; if yours does not, you can work it out by subtracting HDL from the total. It gathers into one figure every particle able to clog arteries, including the ones LDL leaves out. It is especially useful when triglycerides are high or when the sample was not taken fasting.

## Same number, two different plans

Picture two neighbors from the Tidwell area, both with an LDL of 130. One is 32, does not smoke and has no family history of heart trouble. The other is 55, has diabetes and a brother who had a heart attack young. For the first, the plan may be habit changes and a repeat test down the road. For the second, that same LDL sits well above target.

What moves the line is overall risk, which includes:

- Age
- Diabetes or prediabetes
- High blood pressure
- Smoking or vaping
- A parent or sibling who had a heart attack or stroke at a young age
- Kidney disease
- Excess weight, especially around the belly

That is why a result "in range" does not always mean "all good," and one "out of range" does not always mean medication. ## Misreadings we hear all the time

- **"My total came back at 190, so I'm fine."** An acceptable total can hide a high LDL with a very low HDL. The total alone says little.
- **"I'm thin, it can't be me."** High cholesterol also runs in families. Some lean, active people have very high LDL because of their genes.
- **"I ate before coming in, but just a little."** A sweetened coffee or a pastry is enough to move triglycerides. If you ate, say so: the result gets read differently or repeated.
- **"I was given pills once, then my numbers were normal, so I quit."** If they were normal, the medicine was likely the reason. Talk with the clinic's medical team before changing or stopping any treatment.

## What moves each number

Not every habit affects every line equally:

- **To lower LDL,** cut back on saturated fat (food fried in lard, cold cuts, fatty meats, too much cream and cheese) and add soluble fiber: oatmeal, beans, lentils, nopales and fruit with the skin on.
- **To lower triglycerides,** the biggest payoff comes from cutting liquid sugar (sodas, juices, sweetened aguas frescas, syrupy coffee drinks), alcohol and large servings of white flour.
- **To raise HDL,** what works best is regular movement and quitting smoking. A brisk walk on most days already counts.

Triglycerides often respond within weeks; LDL takes longer, and when genetics play a strong role, habits are sometimes not enough. ## How often to check it

It depends on age and risk. As a general guide, the U.S. National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute ([NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/blood-cholesterol/diagnosis)) suggests every five years for younger adults without risk factors, and more often as the years go by. With diabetes, high blood pressure or known high cholesterol, the usual pace is at least once a year, or sooner after a treatment is started or adjusted. Men who have gone years without a checkup can use our [men's health checkup](/services/salud-hombre), which covers cholesterol along with blood pressure and glucose.

## When not to wait for a visit

High cholesterol has no symptoms, but what it leads to over time does. If you or someone near you has severe chest pain, sudden shortness of breath, a drooping face, a weak arm or trouble speaking, call 911 right then: not a drive on your own, and not a trip to our waiting room.
