**One friend swears the pill made her gain weight, a sister-in-law says the shot stopped her period, and someone in the family group chat recommends the implant as if it were right for everybody. With so many versions going around, plenty of women end up with no method at all, or with one they quit after two months.** This guide will not tell you which method is best, because no single method is best for everyone. Instead it walks through the questions worth asking yourself before deciding, so you arrive at the visit with your own list and leave with a method that actually fits your week.

## The rule that helps most: the best one is the one you will use properly

On the [contraceptive methods](/services/anticonceptivos) page of Clínica Hispana Airline we say it plainly: a pill forgotten three times a month protects less than an injection given on schedule. The effectiveness numbers printed on leaflets assume perfect use. Real life brings rotating shifts, trips, sick kids and nights when you fall asleep on the couch.

That is why the first question is a practical one, not a medical one: how much can you commit to a routine?

## Five questions to ask yourself before the visit

**1. Can you take something every day at the same time?** If your schedule is steady and you already take a daily pill, adding one more is usually easy. If you work nights one week and days the next, or your life runs at full speed, a method that does not rely on daily memory will give you more peace of mind.

**2. When would you like to have a baby, if at all?** Wanting to wait one year is not the same as wanting no more children. With some methods fertility comes back right after you stop; with others it can take several months, as sometimes happens with the shot. If you are thinking about getting pregnant soon, say so from the start.

**3. What does your health say?** Before prescribing, the medical team at Clínica Hispana Airline reviews your blood pressure, whether you smoke and your age, migraines with aura, any history of clots, and whether you are breastfeeding. Some of those factors rule out estrogen-containing methods. The point is not to scare you; it is to choose well the first time.

**4. How do you feel about changes in bleeding?** Some methods make periods lighter or stop them for a while; others bring irregular spotting during the first months. Some women do not mind; for others it is a constant worry. Knowing ahead of time prevents surprises.

**5. Do you need nobody else to know?** In some situations discretion matters a lot. A method that leaves no boxes in the bathroom may be what suits you best, and it is perfectly fine to bring that up during the visit.

## Pill or shot: the most common question in the visit

These are the two methods handled directly at the clinic, so they deserve a closer look.

**The pill** has the advantage of keeping you in control: you can stop whenever you want and fertility returns quickly. The downside is consistency. If you miss one, it helps to know beforehand what to do, and that gets explained when it is prescribed. Some pills contain estrogen and some do not; which one is right for you depends on the assessment.

**The shot** removes the daily worry and fits well into lives with shifting schedules. What it asks in return is not letting the date of the next injection slip by: saving it in your phone with an alarm helps a lot. If you want to get pregnant later on, keep in mind that fertility may not come back right away.

After the assessment, at the [clinic's pharmacy](/services/farmacia) you buy right there what the medical team prescribed, along with an explanation of how to start and what to do if you forget something.

## The first months: patience, not endurance

When you start a hormonal method you may notice spotting between periods, tender breasts or mild mood changes. These usually settle over time. What you do not have to do is put up with months of feeling unwell in silence: if the method does not agree with you or the bleeding does not calm down, it gets changed. Write down what you notice (bleeding dates, headaches, how you feel) and bring it to the follow-up; those notes help more than memory does.

## Before you start: ruling out pregnancy

If you have had unprotected sex since your last period, it is worth confirming you are not pregnant before beginning a new method. The [pregnancy test](/services/prueba-embarazo) is done at the clinic, with urine or blood, and the result is explained to you in your language. That way you start with complete peace of mind.

## What to bring to your family planning visit

- The date of your last period
- The methods you have tried and why you stopped them
- The list of medicines and supplements you take
- Whether you smoke, get migraines, or have relatives who had blood clots
- Your pregnancy plans, even if they are still vague

With that information, the medical team at Clínica Hispana Airline can offer you real options instead of a one-size-fits-all prescription. If you are also due for a Pap smear or have any discomfort, it can be checked in the same visit through the [gynecology](/services/ginecologia) service.

## Warning signs that call for 911 or the emergency room

Whatever method you use, do not wait for an appointment if any of these happen:

- Very heavy bleeding with dizziness or fainting
- Severe pelvic pain that starts suddenly
- You might be pregnant and have sharp pain low on one side, or you are bleeding
- High fever together with pelvic pain
- Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden pain and swelling in one leg, or a very bad headache with changes in your vision, especially if you use a hormonal method
